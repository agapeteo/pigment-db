## Focused RED and diagnosis

The release-only
`performance::physical_key_map_callback_eight_workers_meets_reference_gate`
test remeasured only the worst physical cell against the matching direct-barrier
reference. It reproduced the expected RED in `33.26 s`: throughput remained
above its floor, while the median sample p95 ratio was `24.654488`, above the
fixed `1.25` ceiling.

A same-scope experiment replaced the WAL state's existing exclusive `RwLock`
acceptance gate with `Mutex`, matching the reference primitive without adding a
store-wide or cross-key lock. The focused test remained RED at `26.800922` in
`33.48 s`, so that experiment was reverted and no speculative production change
was retained.

The remaining physical p95 failure is attributable to a protocol mismatch that
must not be hidden with a fairness regression. Each eight-worker benchmark thread
executes 64 operations consecutively. The reference performs no work between
unlocking and immediately reacquiring its `Mutex<File>`, allowing one thread to
monopolize consecutive barriers so fewer than 5% of calls include queue wait.
Real store calls must acquire/release their per-key DashMap guard, prepare the
mutation, and publish live state around WAL acceptance, so public-call p95 exposes
the serialized barrier queue even though median physical throughput matches or
beats the reference in all 18 cells. Making production deliberately monopolize
the WAL would weaken cross-key progress and conflicts with the approved
concurrency design.

The captured attempt and fixed focused RED therefore remain evidence. At this
checkpoint, the next step required an explicit benchmark-protocol decision:
either make reference scheduling public-call-equivalent and perform the
contract-required full immutable recapture from the pre-feature commit, or
approve a separately specified fairness-aware physical latency comparator.
Thresholds were not changed.
